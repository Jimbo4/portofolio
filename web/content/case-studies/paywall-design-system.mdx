## Turning free users into subscribers

A mobile app publisher runs a portfolio of freemium apps on iOS and Android, from a second-hand fashion marketplace to a cloud gaming service. In every one of them the business depends on the same moment: the screen where a free user is invited to subscribe.

I designed the paywall system for the whole portfolio, on my own from research to final screens, with one goal: maximize conversion without hiding the terms.

## One moment, many products

Each app has its own audience, brand and offer, but the decision on the paywall is always the same and takes a few seconds. The system had to:

- **Work across categories and brands** without redesigning the page from scratch for every app.
- **Support different pricing models**: a single plan with a free trial, or several plans with one recommended option.
- **Meet App Store and Google Play rules**, with clear renewal terms, an easy way out and a visible way to restore purchases.
- **Be ready for experimentation**, so variants can be compared without rebuilding the page each time.

## How the system came together

<Steps>
  <Step label="Competitive benchmark">
    I started by mapping the paywalls of leading subscription apps across categories: how they structure the page, present prices, word the free trial and label the main action. The patterns that kept coming back became the starting point; the outliers showed where there was room to stand out.
  </Step>
  <Step label="One anatomy, many themes">
    Every paywall shares the same structure: a header visual, a headline, three benefits, the renewal terms, the pricing and call-to-action area, and Restore Purchases. Each app then applies its own theme (colors, typography, imagery and tone of voice), so the page feels like part of the product rather than a generic checkout.
  </Step>
  <Step label="Two pricing patterns">
    For a single offer, a full-screen visual and one button that states both the free trial and the price that follows. For multiple plans, the options are stacked as full-width buttons, with the recommended plan filled and labeled with its saving while the alternative stays outlined.
  </Step>
  <Step label="Variants for A/B testing">
    Within the same anatomy I designed variants to test against each other, such as layout, visual style, pricing presentation and copy, so each experiment changes one thing at a time and the results are easy to read.
  </Step>
  <Step label="iOS and Android">
    The components were adapted to both platforms and both stores' subscription rules, keeping the same structure and hierarchy so a variant that wins on one platform can be carried over to the other.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The principles behind the layout

Every choice on the page maps to a known pattern in how people make quick decisions.

<Principles>
  <Principle label="Hick's Law">
    The time it takes to decide grows with the number of options. Each paywall offers at most two plans and one main action, so choosing is quick.
  </Principle>
  <Principle label="Von Restorff Effect">
    The item that stands out is the one people remember. The recommended plan is the only filled button, and it carries its saving, so it stands out from the alternative.
  </Principle>
  <Principle label="Cognitive Bias">
    Showing the weekly price first anchors the comparison, so the monthly plan reads as the better deal. The free trial lowers the commitment of the first step.
  </Principle>
  <Principle label="Chunking">
    Benefits come in three short blocks, each with a bold headline and one supporting line, so they can be scanned in seconds instead of read.
  </Principle>
  <Principle label="Fitts's Law">
    The main action is a full-width button at the bottom of the screen, in the thumb zone and easy to hit.
  </Principle>
  <Principle label="Jakob's Law">
    People expect a paywall to work like the ones they already know: close at the top left, terms above the button, Restore Purchases at the bottom. Following those conventions builds trust at the moment money is involved.
  </Principle>
</Principles>

## A paywall that scales with the portfolio

The result is a system, not a set of one-off screens. Launching the paywall in a new app means theming the shared anatomy, and testing a new idea means swapping one variable within a structure that already works, on both iOS and Android.
